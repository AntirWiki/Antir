![[elfref.png|right|500]]Elves, despite common reputation, are actually mortal. However, their centuries-long lifespan lends them a perspective separate from most other peoples. The majority of elves who live among other mortals find that after a few centuries, the constant cycles of growth and loss become overwhelming. Most of these elves eventually retire to elven communities or find peace in solitude, a phenomenon known as "waning". Some elves return to society after a period of waning, living in a decades-long cycle of activity and rest.

Many elves have a fondness for and connection to nature, given the slow and long-lasting nature of plant life. This also lends some a powerful connection to the spirits, who typically change much slower than mortals.

Elves originate on the continent of [[Avhalae]], across the ocean to the west of [[Antir]]. Most fled their ancestral homes when the undead [[Blight]] invaded the continent, though occasionally an elf from Antir - especially those reaching the last few centuries of life - will return to Avhalae to help rebuild their ancient homeland and fight against the undead horde.

Elven children are born immobile and tiny - small enough to fit in their parents' palms. The first years of their life are spent stationary, growing extremely slowly, taking in nothing but water and sunlight. Some believe that this time is when the elf develops the mental and physical complexity required for their great lifespans. Elven children in this stage are often referred to as "seedlings".

Elves' children with people of other ancestries, and the children of their children, are called “aiuvarin”, an elven word that refers to a poem about leaves that fall too quickly to the ground.

# Wood Elves
Wood elves are a subculture of elves that originated following the elven migration to Antir. They are essentially a more reclusive, more nature focused people than standard elves. They often live in small villages of 50-300 members. They are very in tune with the world of nature and spirits. They are visually still elves, though many have tinges of green in their skin and hair. Those that are especially spiritual often manifest aspects of Spring (ex. small floral blooms appearing when they walk, small branches and vines growing on their bodies, etc.). 

Wood elves do not generally spend time amongst civilization. It is not uncommon for a wood elf to never set foot in a place like Zauberei or Catsi Crossing. Where their high elf cousins may spend their younger years amongst the rest of the mortal peoples, then returning to rural elven settlements in their waning periods, wood elves take it a step further. They spend their younger years in the small villages, then live out their waning periods in complete isolation, roaming the woods and becoming more and more spirit-like in their continued existence. Occasionally wood elves will choose to spend their waning periods with a handful of companions, but this is rare.

Wood elves are tightly connected to the god [[Soln]]. They are strongly connected to the Fae Court of Spring, and worship the spirits of nature and the hunting god as equals.
