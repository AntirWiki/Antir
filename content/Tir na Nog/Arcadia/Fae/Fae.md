Fae are [[Spirits]] created by ideas about the natural world and its inhabitants - including sentient beings. Fae often represent twisted or idealized forms of real creatures, like a stag with golden antlers, a tree that sings prophecy, or a personification of a local river. For sentient beings, this could be a person that can swap faces, a great hunter that can never miss a shot, or a woman so beautiful she drives mortals mad. They can also represent forces of nature, like a living storm, a roaring wildfire, or a roaming pack of boulders.

The fae are nature spirits, often tricksters or hunters. Despite the fact that many fae resemble mortals, their minds are almost all completely alien to mortal thought processes.

Due to their connection to nature, fae are the most common spirit to inhabit the mortal plane. Some fae - mostly animal and plant spirits - exist fully in the mortal world, never entering Tir na Nog at all. Those that live in the spirit plane are usually found in the realm of [[Tir na Nog#Arcadia|Arcadia]].

The High Fae - the most powerful and intelligent - have a series of noble ranks and titles so complex that all mortal attempts at cataloguing it have failed. Mortals typically refer to a High Fae of sufficient rank and power as an "archfey", though this title is rarely used by the fae themselves. 

Fae cannot be permanently killed by typical means - when one does die, it typically reappears in Arcadia, either during its associated season (if it has one) or simply after some time has passed.

# The Fae Courts
The vast majority of fae fall into one of the four seasonal courts of Arcadia. Those who do not are typically powerful enough to resist pressures from the nobility to pick a side, or unintelligent lesser fae who cannot comprehend politics.

## Spring
The Spring fae center around birth, growth, and renewed life. Amongst the courts they are peacemakers, helping broker the gentle transition from Winter to Summer every year.

With the disappearance of Autumn from most of Antir, the Spring court has also become more mercantile, facilitating trade between Summer and Winter.

## Summer
The summer courts represent the vigor of life in its prime, adventure, and warmth. Summer fae are often boisterous, eager, and dramatic.

Since the diminishing of Autumn in Antir, the Summer Court fights an annual war to prevent the onset of Winter. They always lose eventually (since, once the summer ends, they stop regenerating after death), but Summer does not end until they are defeated, which can range anywhere from late September to early December.

## Autumn
The Autumn court represents the bounty of the harvest, but also decay and death.

The court is greatly diminished in Antir since the [[Autumn King]] was banished to [[Taluvhas]] after the Black Autumn of 1077 BR. Those few who remain in Antir are generally distrusted by fae of the other courts.

Before the Autumn King's banishment, the court served as the brokers and merchants of the fae, making deals with any who would trade and accepting almost anything as payment. Since their decline, that role has been partially taken over by the Spring Court.

Currently, the Autumn Court rules Taluvhas on the mortal plane as an imperial government under the Autumn King.

## Winter
The winter court represents stagnation and the status quo, and the stillness of a land bereft of growth.

With the Autumn Court gone from Antir, the Winter Court must fight each year to bring their season to the continent. They always win eventually, since they begin to regenerate after death when winter begins, but their victory can come anywhere from late September to early December.

They have a more cordial relationship with Spring, and peacefully hand over control when their season ends - so they can begin preparations for next year's war with Summer.
